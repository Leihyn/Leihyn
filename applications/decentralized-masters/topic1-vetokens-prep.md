## The Presentation

### 1. Start With the Problem (30 seconds)

**The analogy:** Imagine you buy shares in a company, but those shares give you zero say in how the company is run. The CEO could change the product, raise prices, dilute your shares — and you just sit there watching. That's what most DeFi governance tokens feel like. You hold the token, but there's no real reason to keep holding it. People buy, dump, price drops, everyone loses.

**The question to pose:** So how do you make people actually want to hold a token long-term instead of dumping it the second they get it?

### 2. What veTokens Are (1-2 minutes)

**The core analogy:** veTokens are like a gym membership with commitment rewards.

- Anyone can walk into a gym and pay per visit (holding a regular token)
- But if you sign a 1-year contract, you get priority access to equipment, discounts on classes, and a vote on what new equipment the gym buys (locking your token to get veTokens)
- The longer your contract, the more perks you get
- You can't cancel early — your commitment is real

**The mechanics (keep it simple):**

- "ve" stands for "vote-escrowed"
- You take a governance token (like CRV from Curve Finance) and lock it for a period — anywhere from a few weeks to 4 years
- In return, you get veCRV — a non-transferable token that represents your locked position
- Longer lock = more veCRV = more power and rewards
- Lock 1,000 CRV for 4 years? You get 1,000 veCRV. Lock for 1 year? You get 250 veCRV. Your voting power decays as your lock approaches expiration.

**Why it's non-transferable matters:** You can't sell your veTokens. This means you can't game the system by borrowing voting power for a single vote and then selling it off. You have skin in the game.

### 3. The Three Benefits to an Investor (2-3 minutes)

#### Benefit 1: Governance Power (You Actually Get a Say)

**Analogy:** It's like the difference between being a renter and a homeowner in a neighborhood association. Renters live there, but homeowners vote on the rules.

- veCRV holders vote on which liquidity pools get CRV emissions (rewards)
- This is called "gauge voting" — you point rewards toward pools you care about
- This is genuinely powerful: if you're providing liquidity in a specific pool, you can vote to direct more rewards to YOUR pool
- Protocols actually bribe veCRV holders to vote for their pools (this is a real market — Convex, Votium)

#### Benefit 2: Boosted Rewards (More Yield for Committed Capital)

**Analogy:** Think of it like a loyalty card at your favorite restaurant. First-time visitors pay full price. But regulars with the loyalty card? They get 2.5x portions for the same price.

- On Curve, if you provide liquidity AND hold veCRV, your LP rewards get boosted up to 2.5x
- Without veCRV: you earn base rate. With max veCRV: you earn up to 2.5x that rate
- This means committed investors earn significantly more than tourists who are just passing through

#### Benefit 3: Revenue Sharing (Real Yield)

**Analogy:** This is like owning a franchise. You don't just hold a token hoping the price goes up — you actually receive a cut of the business's revenue.

- veCRV holders receive a share of trading fees generated by Curve
- This is real yield — it comes from actual protocol usage, not from printing new tokens
- Every swap on Curve generates fees. A portion of those fees goes to veCRV holders.
- This makes it one of the few tokens where holding it generates income, not just speculative upside

### 4. Why This Model Is Powerful (30 seconds — the closer)

**Bring it together:**

- veTokens solved the "farm and dump" problem. Before veTokens, people would earn governance tokens and immediately sell them. Price tanks, everyone loses.
- The ve model says: "If you want the best rewards and real governance power, you have to commit." It aligns incentives between the protocol and its users.
- Curve pioneered this, and now dozens of protocols use the model: Velodrome (Optimism), Aerodrome (Base), Balancer (veBAL), Frax (veFXS)
- It turned passive token holders into active, committed stakeholders

### 5. Quick Summary to Land the Ending

Three things to remember about veTokens:
1. Lock your tokens, get voting power — the longer you lock, the more power you get
2. Boosted rewards — committed investors earn more than everyone else
3. Real revenue sharing — you earn from actual protocol fees, not just token inflation

## Gotcha Questions You Might Want to Address

**"What's the risk?"**
- Your tokens are locked. If the market crashes, you can't sell. That's the tradeoff — commitment cuts both ways.
- Your voting power decays over time as your lock period shortens. You need to re-lock to maintain max power.

**"Is this only Curve?"**
- Curve pioneered it, but the model has been adopted widely: Velodrome, Aerodrome, Balancer, Frax, and others.

**"What's the Convex angle?"**
- Convex Finance lets you deposit CRV and get boosted rewards without locking for 4 years yourself. They pool everyone's CRV, lock it, and share the boost. It's like a veToken co-op.
