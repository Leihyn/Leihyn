## THE PROBLEM

Imagine you buy shares in a company.

But these shares give you no voting rights. No dividends. And the CEO can dilute your ownership whenever they want.

Why would you hold these shares? You wouldn't. You'd sell them the moment you could.

That's exactly what most DeFi governance tokens feel like. You earn them, you hold them, but there's no real reason to keep holding. So what do people do? They dump them. The price crashes. This is called the "farm and dump" problem — and it has killed countless protocols.

So the question is: How do you make people want to hold a token long-term instead of dumping it immediately?

Someone figured it out. Curve Finance invented veTokens.

Let me explain veTokens with a simple analogy.

Think about a gym membership.

If you're a casual visitor, you pay per visit. You get access to the basic equipment, you pay full price, but you don't have any say in how the gym is run. ## HOW veTOKENS WORK

"ve" stands for "vote-escrowed."

You take a governance token — like CRV from Curve Finance — and you lock it for a set period. It could be a few weeks, or up to four years.

In return, you get veCRV — a special token that represents your locked position.

The important thing is: the longer you lock, the more veCRV you get.

And veCRV is non-transferable. You can't sell it. You can't game the system by borrowing voting power for one vote and then dumping it. This means you have real skin in the game.

## BENEFIT 1: GOVERNANCE POWER

So what are the benefits of veTokens?

The first benefit is governance power — real voting power.

Let me use another analogy. Think about a residential estate. You have homeowners and you have renters. Renters get to use the houses, the parking lot, the roads, the parks, all the amenities in the estate. But when it comes to voting on how the estate is run? They don't have a say. Only homeowners vote on how the estate is governed.

In the case of Curve Finance, veCRV holders are the homeowners. They vote on which liquidity pools receive CRV rewards. This is called "gauge voting" — you can vote to direct more rewards toward the pools you want.

Here's something interesting: other protocols actually pay veCRV holders to vote for their pools. There's a whole market for this — platforms like Convex and Votium. Real money changing hands for your vote.

This gives real power to the holders.
