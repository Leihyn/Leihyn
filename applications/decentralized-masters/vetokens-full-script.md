## WHAT ARE veTOKENS? — THE GYM ANALOGY (60-90 seconds)

Let me explain veTokens with a simple analogy.

Think about a gym membership.

If you're a casual visitor, you can walk in and pay per visit. You get access to the basic equipment, you pay full price, and you have no say in how the gym is run. If you don't like something, tough luck — you're just passing through.

But what if you sign a one-year contract?

Now you get priority access to the best equipment. You get discounts on classes. And here's the key part — you get to vote on what new equipment the gym buys.

The longer your contract, the more perks you get. And you can't cancel early. Your commitment is real.

*[Beat.]*

That's exactly how veTokens work.

"ve" stands for "vote-escrowed."

You take a governance token — like CRV from Curve Finance — and you lock it for a set period. Could be a few weeks, could be up to four years.

In return, you get veCRV — a special token that represents your locked position.

Here's the important part: **the longer you lock, the more veCRV you get.**

Lock 1,000 CRV for four years? You get 1,000 veCRV.

Lock that same 1,000 CRV for just one year? You only get 250 veCRV.

And veCRV is non-transferable. You can't sell it. You can't game the system by borrowing voting power for one vote and then dumping it. You have real skin in the game.

---

## BENEFIT 1: GOVERNANCE POWER (45-60 seconds)

So what do you actually get for locking your tokens?

Let's start with benefit number one: **real governance power.**

Here's another analogy. Think about the difference between being a renter and a homeowner in a neighborhood.

Renters live there. They use the streets, the parks, the amenities. But when the neighborhood association meets to vote on new rules? Renters don't get a say.

Homeowners vote.

veCRV holders are the homeowners of Curve Finance.

They vote on which liquidity pools receive CRV rewards. This is called "gauge voting." You're literally pointing rewards toward the pools you care about.

And here's where it gets interesting: if you're providing liquidity in a specific pool, you can vote to direct more rewards to YOUR pool. You're increasing your own yield.

It gets even better. Other protocols actually PAY veCRV holders to vote for their pools. There's a whole market for this — platforms like Convex and Votium. Real money, changing hands, for your vote.

That's real power.

---

## BENEFIT 2: BOOSTED REWARDS (45-60 seconds)

Benefit number two: **boosted rewards.**

Think about a loyalty card at your favorite restaurant.

First-time visitors pay full price and get a normal portion. But regulars who have the loyalty card? They get two-and-a-half times the portion — for the same price.

That's how veCRV works on Curve.

If you provide liquidity on Curve without holding veCRV, you earn the base rate. Nothing wrong with that.

But if you provide liquidity AND hold veCRV? Your rewards get boosted up to 2.5x.

Same pool. Same amount deposited. But committed investors earn significantly more than tourists who are just passing through.

The protocol is literally saying: "If you commit to us long-term, we'll reward you more." And that's fair.

---

## BENEFIT 3: REVENUE SHARING (45-60 seconds)

Benefit number three — and this is the big one: **real revenue sharing.**

Most governance tokens are like lottery tickets. You hold them hoping the price goes up. That's it. Pure speculation.

veTokens are different. They're more like owning a franchise.

Every time someone swaps tokens on Curve, the protocol generates trading fees. Real revenue from real usage.

And a portion of those fees goes directly to veCRV holders.

This isn't inflation. This isn't the protocol printing new tokens out of thin air. This is actual revenue, from actual users, flowing to actual token holders.

That's what we call "real yield." And it's one of the only models in DeFi where just holding a token generates genuine income.

---

## WHY THIS MODEL WORKS (30-45 seconds)

So let's bring it all together. Why does the ve model work?

Before veTokens, DeFi had a massive problem. Protocols would distribute governance tokens as rewards. People would earn them, dump them immediately, and the price would crash. Death spiral.

The ve model fixed this.

It says: "You want the best rewards? Commit. You want real governance power? Commit. You want a share of the revenue? Commit."

It aligns incentives between the protocol and its users. Everyone wins together.

Curve pioneered this model, and now it's everywhere: Velodrome on Optimism, Aerodrome on Base, Balancer, Frax — dozens of protocols have adopted it.

It turned passive token holders into active, committed stakeholders.
